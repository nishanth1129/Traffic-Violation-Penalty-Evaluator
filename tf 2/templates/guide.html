Driver Guide

Everything they didn't teach in driver's ed.

A pragmatic guide to staying out of trouble - and what to do when you can't.

Stay safe on the road

How can I prevent the most common citations?
Use cruise control on highways, give yourself extra time so you don't speed, mount your phone before driving, full-stop at every stop sign, and check all your lights monthly. These five habits cover ~80% of common tickets.
What should I do if I'm pulled over?
Pull over safely, turn off the engine, keep hands on the wheel, and politely identify yourself. Don't volunteer information. You can decline searches. Be respectful but exercise your rights.
Should I admit fault?
No. Anything you say can be used in court. Be polite but say only what's necessary. "I'm not sure" is a perfectly fine answer to most questions about how fast you were going.
Can I refuse a breathalyzer?
Almost every state has implied-consent laws - refusal usually triggers automatic license suspension. Refusal can also be used as evidence at trial. Consult an attorney for your state's specifics.
What's the difference between civil and criminal traffic offenses?
Civil infractions (most tickets) result in fines and points. Criminal offenses (DUI, reckless, hit-and-run) can mean jail, probation, and a permanent record. Treat criminal charges with serious legal counsel.

If you got a ticket

Should I just pay the ticket?
Paying admits guilt, adds points, and raises insurance. For minor tickets it's often easier. For anything serious, contesting or hiring a lawyer can save thousands over time.
How do I contest a ticket?
Plead not guilty by the deadline, request the officer's appearance, and prepare your case (photos, witness statements, dashcam). Many tickets get dismissed when the officer doesn't show up.
Will my insurance go up?
Most major violations raise rates 20-40% for 3 years. Minor tickets may not affect rates if you have a clean prior record. Check with your insurer about forgiveness programs.
Can I take traffic school?
Most states allow one course every 12-18 months to remove or prevent points from a single ticket. It usually keeps insurance rates stable. Costs $25-$100 and takes 4-8 hours online.
When do I need a lawyer?
Always for criminal charges (DUI, reckless, hit-and-run). Strongly consider for: CDL holders, accumulating multiple tickets, accidents involving injury, or any potential license suspension.

Top 5 driving habits that save money

1

Set cruise on highways

Eliminates speed drift, the #1 reason for highway tickets.

2

Mount your phone

Hands-free is legal in all 50 states. Holding it is not.

3

Full stop. Always.

Count to two at every stop sign and right-on-red.

4

Check lights monthly

A burned-out tail light is the easiest stop for an officer.
